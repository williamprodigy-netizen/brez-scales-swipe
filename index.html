What matters

The price anchor is the hook, and it is a price he is no longer charging

“This used to be part of my $5,000 course. Now you can learn it for free.” A free offer normally reads as worth nothing. Attaching it to a real, abandoned price gives the free thing a receipt. He never has to defend the $5,000 — he is not charging it. It is the cleanest free-lead-magnet framing in this file.

He answers &ldquo;what's the catch&rdquo; as a page section

The second H2 is literally “How Is This Free?”. The objection that a $5,000-anchored freebie creates is the very next thing the page handles. Most funnels create that suspicion and leave it running.

A two-minute callback promise, in writing

The application confirmation reads “Congrats for applying. Expect to receive a text and a call in the next 2 minutes”. That is a commitment the sales team must then hit, and it makes the speed-to-lead standard visible to the lead. worth stealing

Forced consumption stated as an instruction, not enforced by the player

The ERT page says “do NOT apply before watching the video below”. No locked player, no timer — just an instruction. Cheaper than Brook Hiddink's enforced version, and it filters on compliance rather than patience.

Two of six funnel URLs are dead

/en/free-training-org and the LeadConnector booking widget both return 404. The organic training path and one booking route are broken as of 18 August. Either mid-migration, or leaking traffic.
